This guide is Reroute's own plain-language summary of Regulation (EC) No 261/2004 and related EU case law. It is not legal advice. Always check the official text on EUR-Lex.

## Article 3 — Which flights are covered

The regulation applies to every flight departing from an airport in the EU, Iceland, Norway or Switzerland, whatever the airline. It also applies to flights arriving in the EU from outside when the operating airline is an EU carrier. A flight from New York to Amsterdam operated by an EU airline is covered; the same route on a non-EU airline is not. Flights between two non-EU airports are never covered.

## Article 4 — Denied boarding

If you are denied boarding against your will (for example because of overbooking) and you presented yourself for check-in on time with valid documents, you are entitled to compensation, a choice between refund or re-routing, and care (meals, accommodation if needed).

## Article 5 — Cancellation

When a flight is cancelled you are entitled to a refund or re-routing and to care while waiting. Compensation is also due unless the airline informed you at least 14 days before departure, or offered an acceptable re-route close to your original schedule. With 7 to 13 days' notice, a re-route departing no more than two hours earlier and arriving less than four hours later removes the right to compensation. With less than 7 days' notice, the re-route must depart no more than one hour earlier and arrive less than two hours later.

## Article 5(3) — Extraordinary circumstances

The airline does not owe compensation if it proves the cancellation or delay was caused by extraordinary circumstances that could not have been avoided even if all reasonable measures had been taken. Typical examples are severe weather, air traffic control restrictions or strikes by air traffic controllers, security risks, political instability, and bird strikes. Technical problems discovered during normal maintenance are generally not extraordinary. Strikes by the airline's own staff are not extraordinary circumstances according to the Court of Justice of the EU (case C-28/20). Care and assistance (meals, hotel) must be provided even when circumstances are extraordinary.

## Article 6 — Long delays and care

For long delays at departure the airline must offer care: meals and refreshments, two free communications, and hotel accommodation plus transport when an overnight stay becomes necessary. If the delay is at least five hours you may also choose a refund and abandon the trip.

## Delay compensation (Sturgeon ruling)

The Court of Justice of the EU decided in the Sturgeon case (C-402/07) that passengers who reach their final destination three hours or more after the scheduled arrival time are entitled to the same compensation as for a cancellation. What counts is the arrival time at the final destination, defined as the moment at least one aircraft door is opened.

## Article 7 — Compensation amounts

The amount depends on the flight distance, measured as the great-circle distance between departure airport and final destination:
- €250 for flights of 1,500 km or less.
- €400 for all flights within the EU of more than 1,500 km, and for all other flights between 1,500 and 3,500 km.
- €600 for flights of more than 3,500 km that are not within the EU.

Article 7(2) allows the airline to reduce compensation by 50% when it offered re-routing and the arrival delay of the re-routed journey was no more than two hours (≤1,500 km), three hours (1,500–3,500 km or intra-EU) or four hours (longer flights).

## Article 8 — Refund or re-routing

When entitled, you can choose between a refund of the ticket within seven days, re-routing at the earliest opportunity, or re-routing at a later date of your choice, subject to seat availability.

## Article 16 — Complaints and enforcement

First submit your claim to the airline. If the airline rejects it or does not answer within a reasonable time (often 6–8 weeks), you can complain to the National Enforcement Body (NEB) of the country where the incident happened — in the Netherlands this is the Human Environment and Transport Inspectorate (ILT). You can also go to court or use an alternative dispute resolution body. Time limits for court claims depend on the country, ranging from one to several years; in the Netherlands it is generally two years.
